Project Overview

Hotview.com is being designed as a global e-commerce marketplace spanning multiple categories such as properties, cars, services, electronics and fashion. The first phase focuses heavily on real estate, letting users buy, rent, lease, or book shortlet properties and hotels through a single digital experience.

The product is designed to launch in Nigeria, with infrastructure and UX patterns built to support international expansion further down the line. This is an ongoing engagement — the case study below reflects the design direction established so far, not a finished, shipped product.

"Property discovery in most emerging markets is fragmented. Hotview's job is to make it feel like one trustworthy place."

The Business Problem

Property discovery and transactions in many emerging markets are:

  • Fragmented across multiple platforms, with no single source of truth
  • Lacking transparency and verification, which erodes buyer and renter trust
  • Time-consuming and unreliable for people who just want a straight answer

Hotview set out to solve this by creating a centralized, trusted marketplace connecting property owners, agents, and buyers or renters in one place.

My Role & Approach

1. Product & UX Strategy

Translated business goals into user-centered product flows for multiple user types — guests, buyers, agents, and hosts — while planning for scalability beyond real estate into other marketplace categories.

2. Information Architecture & Marketplace Structure

Structured listings for shortlets, rentals, sales, and hotels, with navigation designed for fast browsing, searching, and filtering across very different property types.

3. UI Design & Visual System

  • Designed clean, modern interfaces built to inspire trust in a first-time marketplace
  • Established visual hierarchy for listing cards, pricing, and calls to action
  • Built reusable components designed to extend into future marketplace categories

4. Search, Filters & Discovery

Designed a smart search experience based on location, price, and property type, focused on removing friction from property discovery.

5. Booking, Payments & Trust Signals

Designed booking and payment flows with clarity in mind, and incorporated verified-listing badges, reviews, and agent credibility indicators to build user confidence.

Design Reasoning Notes

Marketplace products live or die on trust. Whenever a decision came down to "faster" versus "more credible" — verification badges, agent profiles, clear pricing — I biased toward credibility, because a marketplace with no trust has no transaction.

Designing for five distinct property types inside one system also meant building components generic enough to extend into cars, electronics, and services later, rather than one-off screens per category. That constraint shaped almost every UI decision on the project.
